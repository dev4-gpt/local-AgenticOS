# Veloce AgenticOS: HeyGen Product Explainer

## Production brief

- **Format:** 16:9 master video, with center-safe composition for 9:16 cutdowns.
- **Target length:** 4 minutes 30 seconds to 5 minutes.
- **Presenter:** HeyGen avatar, direct-to-camera, confident and conversational.
- **Visual rule:** show the real local product, real route names, and approved sample data. Do not show credentials, terminal secrets, private vault content, personal email, or client files.
- **Pacing:** avatar for the hook and transitions; full-screen product capture for demonstrations; return to avatar for the close.
- **Primary CTA:** View the repository and tell us one broken handoff you would turn into a workflow.

## Master script

### 00:00-00:18 - Hook

**Avatar voiceover**

Most people do not have an AI system. They have an AI tab problem.

One model for writing. Another for coding. Notes somewhere else. Research in a browser. Prompts in random files. Every new conversation starts cold, and every useful result has to be carried by hand into the next tool.

So I built the missing layer: Veloce AgenticOS.

**On screen**

Fast cuts of disconnected tabs, then transition into the Veloce Mission Control signal tunnel. Title: `Veloce AgenticOS / AI work needs an operating layer.`

### 00:18-00:43 - What it is

**Avatar voiceover**

Veloce AgenticOS is a private, local-first mission control system for agents, memory, workflows, creation, governance, and delivery.

It is not another chatbot skin. It is a working environment that connects the tools you already use, gives them durable context, and turns repeated work into visible workflows with human approval.

The private dashboard runs on your machine. Public service experiences stay separate from local files, credentials, and execution controls.

**On screen**

Scroll through the six Mission Control chapters: Command, Context, Orchestrate, Create, Govern, Deliver.

### 00:43-01:13 - Command

**Avatar voiceover**

The first layer is Command.

Instead of designing your process around one provider, Veloce gives multiple agents a shared control surface. Claude and Codex can handle serious engineering work. Hermes and OpenClaw can support agent workflows. Local Ollama models can handle lower-cost or private first passes. Other providers can be connected when they have a clear job.

The goal is not to use every model at once. The goal is to route each task to the right worker, with its status and boundary visible.

**On screen**

Show the Command section in the sidebar, then quick views of Claude, Codex, Hermes, OpenClaw, and Local. Avoid entering prompts that expose private conversation history.

### 01:13-01:46 - Context and memory

**Avatar voiceover**

The second layer is Context, and this is where the system becomes more valuable over time.

Goals, journals, project notes, approved research, prompts, and decisions can live in an Obsidian vault as ordinary Markdown. Graphify adds a connected knowledge view, so ideas do not remain isolated documents.

That means the next workflow can begin from durable project memory instead of another blank box. The source remains inspectable, editable, and local.

**On screen**

Open Memory. Show vault status, a safe sample note, then the Graphify panel or sample relationship view. Show Goals and Journal briefly.

### 01:46-02:17 - Orchestrate

**Avatar voiceover**

The third layer is Orchestration.

A useful operating system needs more than answers. It needs movement from intake to decision to execution.

Pipeline turns an idea into a shaped task and an approved build. Paperclip provides an organizational workspace. Agent Kanban and Loop make ownership, review, and completion criteria visible. Ruflo can support scoped multi-agent work where that complexity is justified.

The human approval step stays explicit. Agents can prepare and recommend; important external actions do not silently publish themselves.

**On screen**

Show Pipeline stages, Paperclip status, Agent Kanban, and the Loop acceptance concept. Highlight an approval gate.

### 02:17-03:00 - Create: Flow Lab and Web Studio

**Avatar voiceover**

The fourth layer is Creation.

Flow Lab studies permitted websites, repositories, and recordings at the level of pacing, hierarchy, interaction rhythm, and conversion logic. It turns those observations into an original brief with source attribution and ownership rules.

Web Studio then turns the approved strategy into a website structure, design profile, motion direction, prompt set, and build handoff.

The system now includes a reusable owned-frame sequence workflow. You can define the frame count, format, aspect ratio, and scroll distance, then hand any approved model the same tested canvas pattern with loading, mobile, and reduced-motion fallbacks.

The point is not to copy a reference. The point is to understand why an experience works, then produce an original system using owned assets.

**On screen**

In Flow Lab, select `Immersive operational` and `Owned frame sequence`. Show the sequence contract. Create a sample brief, then hand it to Web Studio. Show the design profile and production prompt.

### 03:00-03:30 - Media and output

**Avatar voiceover**

The creation layer also includes video, voice, music, thumbnails, OpenMontage, and design workspaces.

These modules are provider-aware. If a service needs a paid key or an active login, Veloce should say so instead of pretending the feature is available. Local workflows remain the default where they are capable enough; specialized providers are optional gates for specialized output.

This very explainer can move from a verified product script into an approved HeyGen avatar workflow.

**On screen**

Show Video Studio, OpenMontage, Music, and Thumbnails. Use status labels and sample projects; do not trigger paid generation during the recording.

### 03:30-04:02 - Govern

**Avatar voiceover**

The fifth layer is Governance.

Veloce separates built-in skills, operator-managed connectors, and optional provider services. Integrations show what is configured and what is waiting. Capabilities define what a tool may access and what action it may take.

Secrets stay outside Git. Client work can use isolated keys and budgets. Security release checks look for exposed local paths, private routes, credentials, and unsafe public boundaries before anything is shipped.

This is the difference between collecting AI tools and operating them responsibly.

**On screen**

Show Integrations, Capabilities, the Scroll Asset Sequencing capability, and the security release boundary. Blur any local usernames or filesystem details if they appear.

### 04:02-04:30 - Deliver and use cases

**Avatar voiceover**

The final layer is Delivery.

For an agency, Veloce can turn intake, research, positioning, website production, content, approvals, and reporting into a repeatable client system.

For a founder, it can connect product decisions, market research, engineering, and launch work.

For a student or researcher, it can connect sources, notes, goals, writing, and a searchable knowledge map.

For a professional, it can become a private workspace for learning, portfolio proof, applications, projects, and content.

Different users need different workflows. The operating principles stay the same: durable context, clear routing, human approval, and reusable outcomes.

**On screen**

Show Deliver destinations, then four short use-case title cards: Agency, Founder, Research, Career.

The result is not a finished promise that every provider is free or every integration is automatic. It is a working, inspectable foundation for building better AI operations without turning your business into a pile of disconnected subscriptions.

The repository is public. The private runtime stays yours.

Take a look, and tell me one broken handoff you would turn into a workflow.

## Recording checklist

- Use a sample vault and sample projects.
- Close email, dashboards, terminals, and browser tabs containing credentials.
- Confirm every route shown loads before recording.
- Record the product at 1440p or higher and crop the browser chrome where possible.
- Keep cursor movement deliberate and pause briefly on each status label.
- Add captions and chapter labels; do not rely on the avatar voice alone.
- Put the repository link in the description and the first comment.
